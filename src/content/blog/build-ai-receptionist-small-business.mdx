An AI receptionist is a voice or text agent that answers when your team cannot. It greets the caller, asks your qualifying questions, books a slot in your calendar and sends you a summary. You build it from four parts: a phone number, a voice or chat platform, an automation tool for the logic, and your calendar.

## What an AI receptionist can and cannot do

It handles the repeatable parts of a front desk: answering common questions, collecting a name and job details, checking availability and booking. It should not handle emergencies, complaints, price negotiation or anything that needs judgment. Those go to a person, and the agent should know how to hand off.

## How it works

![Flow diagram of an AI receptionist: call comes in, agent answers, qualifies, checks the calendar, books and notifies your team](/images/blog/build-ai-receptionist-small-business/flow.webp)

1. **The call or text arrives.** Your existing number forwards to the platform when you do not pick up, or always, depending on your setup.
2. **The agent answers.** A voice platform such as Retell AI, Vapi or ElevenLabs handles speech. For texts, an AI model replies through an SMS or WhatsApp provider.
3. **It qualifies the caller.** It asks the questions you wrote: job type, location, urgency.
4. **It checks the calendar.** An automation tool such as n8n or Make looks up free slots in Google Calendar or your booking tool.
5. **It books and confirms.** The appointment is created and the caller gets a confirmation.
6. **Your team is told.** A summary goes to your CRM, email or Slack, with a transcript if you want one.

## What you need

- A business phone number that can forward calls
- A voice or chat platform for the agent
- An automation tool (n8n or Make) to connect the pieces
- A calendar or booking tool with clear availability rules
- A written script: your questions, your services, your opening hours and your handoff rules
- A place to store leads, such as a CRM or Airtable

## Decisions to make first

**What the agent may promise.** Give it facts you have approved: services, areas, hours. Tell it to say it will check and call back for anything else, so it never guesses a price.

**When it hands off.** Define the cases that go straight to a human, such as emergencies or existing clients with a problem.

**What it says about being an AI.** Say so plainly at the start. Callers accept it when the agent is useful.

**Recording and data.** Calls contain personal data. Check your local rules on call recording, texting consent and data privacy, and where your AI and phone providers process data.

## Test it before it takes real calls

Most failures come from small wiring problems, not the AI. Call it yourself many times. Interrupt it, mumble, go off script, ask for a slot that is taken and ask something it should not answer. Fix each failure, then run it on real traffic with a human reviewing the first week of summaries.

## Common questions

**Will callers hang up when they hear an AI?**
Some will. Many do not mind if the agent is quick, polite and gets the job done. A clear handoff to a person helps.

**Can it work with my existing calendar?**
Usually yes, if the calendar has an API or a connector in n8n or Make. Google Calendar and similar tools are the easy cases.

**Does it replace a human receptionist?**
It covers the calls a person cannot take: after hours, busy periods and overflow. A person still handles the complex conversations.
