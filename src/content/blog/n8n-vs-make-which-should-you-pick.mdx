I build in both tools every week, and clients ask the same question before every project: n8n or Make? The answer depends on four things.

## 1. Who will maintain it

Make has a friendlier visual builder. If you or a non-technical teammate will open the scenario later to change a field or fix a filter, Make is easier to hand over.

n8n gives you more control, including code nodes, custom HTTP calls, and sub-workflows. That helps when a developer owns the system, and it gets in the way when nobody on your team writes code.

## 2. How much volume you run

Make charges by operations. A scenario that loops over 500 rows burns 500 operations or more per run, and the bill grows with volume.

n8n charges by workflow execution on the cloud plan, and self-hosting has no per-execution fee. Once you process thousands of records a day, n8n is usually cheaper.

## 3. How complex the logic is

Routers, filters and iterators in Make cover most small business needs. When you need retries with backoff, branching on AI output, merging data from several sources, or custom error handling, n8n handles it with less workaround.

## 4. Where your data lives

Self-hosting n8n keeps data on your own server. That matters for clients with privacy rules. Make runs in its cloud only.

## My rule of thumb

- **Pick Make** for quick builds, simple app-to-app syncing, and teams that will edit the scenarios themselves.
- **Pick n8n** for AI-heavy flows, high volume, complex logic, or when data must stay on your server.
- **Use both** when a client already has Make scenarios that work. I add n8n only for the parts Make does badly.

If you are unsure, describe the process to me and I will tell you which tool fits, even if the answer is neither.
